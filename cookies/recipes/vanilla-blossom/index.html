Vanilla Blossom Cookies

Brown Butter Brown Sugar Sugar Cookies

Makes about 24

A warm, golden sugar cookie: half the butter browned, a third of the sugar brown, sour cream and buttermilk powder for a tender, subtly tangy crumb — and a full tablespoon of vanilla bean paste in bloom over it all.

Ingredients

24 cookies

House rule: amounts always stay in kitchen fractions, even scaled. Round eggs to the nearest whole in practice.

Method

  1. 1

    Brown half the butter

    Brown half the butter over medium heat, about 3 minutes, until deep golden and nutty. Scrape into a bowl and cool to room temperature (soft is fine) so it won't melt the creamed butter later.

    3:00
  2. 2

    Whisk the dry ingredients

    Whisk 2 1/2 cups flour, 1 Tbsp cornstarch, 2 Tbsp buttermilk powder, the baking powder, baking soda, and salt.

  3. 3

    Cream the butter and sugars

    Beat the remaining softened butter with 3/4 cup granulated sugar and 1/2 cup brown sugar until pale and fluffy, about 2 minutes. Add the cooled browned butter and beat until combined.

    2:00
  4. 4

    Add the wet ingredients

    Beat in the egg, yolk, 2 Tbsp sour cream, 1 Tbsp vanilla, and 1/4 tsp almond extract until smooth.

  5. 5

    Combine the dough

    Add the dry mixture and mix on low just until a soft dough forms.

  6. 6

    Chill the dough

    Cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour — the browned butter, sour cream, and brown sugar all encourage spread.

    60:00
  7. 7

    Scoop

    Scoop 1 1/2-Tbsp balls, 2 inches apart; flatten slightly for a more even cookie.

  8. 8

    Bake

    Bake at 350°F for 9–11 minutes, until the edges are set and the cookies are golden but the centers still look soft.

    10:00
  9. 9

    Cool

    Rest on the pan 5 minutes, then cool on a rack.

    5:00

Notes

The full tablespoon of vanilla bean paste is the blossom — flecks and perfume carried by the nutty browned butter, with the 1/4 tsp of almond as the quiet financier echo underneath. These bake golden, not pale; that's correct — just don't overbake. If the dough seems soft after chilling, work in 1–2 Tbsp more flour. Store airtight 3–4 days; dough freezes 2–3 months.
