Property finance · Product design & engineering

HAVEN

The numbers are only part of the work.

Property accounting asks more than whether the numbers add up. Someone needs to know whose money it is, which agreement applies, what has been earned, and what is still owed. I’m building HAVEN to keep those answers connected, from the general ledger and reservation finances to owner statements and trust accounting.

01 · The starting point

The answer depends on more than the transaction.

A vendor asks whether an invoice has been paid. An owner asks why a distribution is smaller this month. A bank deposit arrives with money from several reservations bundled into one amount. Each question starts with a number, but answering it means understanding the relationships behind that number.

My work in vacation rental accounting has made that kind of searching familiar. The individual steps are often straightforward; keeping their context together takes attention. When someone else picks up the task, much of that understanding has to be assembled again.

HAVEN is my attempt to give that understanding a place to live. The foundation needs to know the properties, the people, the agreements, and the books they belong to. The workspaces then give someone a way to use that context without having to reconstruct it each time.

The question behind HAVEN

What would it take to answer the next question without retracing all the earlier work?

01

Keep your place in the work.

Open the record behind a bill, balance, or reservation, then return to the surrounding work with your question still in view.

02

Keep the source close.

Put the invoice, receipt, or payment proof beside the record it supports, where it can actually inform a decision.

03

Show what is still needed.

Make missing evidence, incomplete coding, and pending approval visible before someone tries to move the work forward.

02 · Portfolio & accounting setup

The work starts before the first transaction.

Before a bill can be coded or an owner can be paid, the system needs to understand who and what the work belongs to. I built that structure into HAVEN so it can travel with the accounting.

Properties & ownership

Whose home, and whose share?

Property or unit records connect to individual or entity owners, with ownership percentages and effective dates. Incomplete ownership coverage is visible, and records can be deactivated while their history remains available.

Owner contracts

The agreement belongs with the calculation.

Dated contract versions hold management fees, owner charges, reserve rules, and the properties they cover. A change goes through preview, approval, and activation; historical statement and revenue lines retain the agreement used at the time.

Accounting setup

Give recurring decisions a home.

Fiscal periods, close policies, tax setup, materiality settings, and document numbering are maintained as part of the system. Policy changes keep an approval history, and issued document numbers are not reused.

Opening conversion

Bring the unfinished work along.

Opening bank cash, the general ledger’s trust liability, and individual owner trust balances can be staged alongside unpaid bills, open receivables, and outstanding bank items. Conversion checks that the trust totals agree before confirming the starting point and creating the opening records.

03 · General ledger & calculation rules

The books need a place of their own.

There are moments when someone needs to work directly with the accounts: make an adjustment, investigate a balance, or check how a recurring charge was calculated. HAVEN gives that work its own desk.

Chart of accounts

A maintained accounting structure.

Accounts can be created, searched, edited, activated, deactivated, or replaced while prior entries remain traceable. Account rules distinguish ordinary posting from protected control accounts.

Journals & trial balance

Follow a balance back to its entries.

Manual journals carry property, owner, reservation, and vendor context through review and posting. The trial balance opens into account activity and its journals. A correction uses a linked reversal, preserving the original entry.

Posting templates

See the effect before applying the rule.

Reusable debit and credit rules have effective versions, an exact accounting preview, and an approval step. Revenue and recurring-charge records retain the rule version used, so the calculation can be explained later.

Recurring charges & commissions

Routine work should not depend on memory.

Flat, percentage, per-stay, and per-night charges can be previewed, generated, reviewed, and posted, with missed periods brought back into view. Commission runs separately retain their rules, reservation assignments, calculation preview, and approval history.

04 · Accounts payable & payment evidence

What counts as finished?

The invoice-to-close pilot is one complete route through that larger system. An approved bill, a recorded payment, and a reconciled bank transaction answer different questions. HAVEN follows a USD vendor invoice through those stages, showing what supports each one and what still needs to happen.

  1. 01Capture & codeKeep the invoice and check the line totals.
  2. 02Review & approveHave another person review the bill.
  3. 03Prepare paymentPrepare the payment records and note the external handoff.
  4. 04Confirm at bankImport bank activity and accept the matching transaction.
  5. 05Reconcile & closeInclude the bank match in an approved, locked reconciliation.

The current path uses CSV bank imports and a payment handoff outside HAVEN. Preparing the paperwork does not, by itself, mean the money has been sent.

Bill Entry Desk

Start with what someone needs to know.

When there are several bills to work through, opening each one just to find its status becomes work of its own. The Bill Entry Desk shows the vendor, amount, due date, payment state, and review status in the queue. Filters help bring missing evidence or possible duplicates to the surface.

Inline Review

Look closer without losing your place.

An amount on a bill may need to be split between accounts or checked against the original invoice. Expanding the row brings the coding, totals, related records, and document together. The design gives the person doing the review room to investigate, then return to the rest of the queue.

  • Compare the invoice total with the coded lines.
  • See which fields or documents still need attention.
  • Follow the related vendor, property, and financial records.
  • Save the review and move on to the next bill.
Reading the document

A useful suggestion still needs someone to check it.

Extracting an invoice number or amount can spare someone another round of typing. It can also give an incorrect value the appearance of certainty. HAVEN keeps the suggested fields beside their source, with confidence indicators and a review step before the information becomes a bill.

When a person corrects a field, the original value, replacement, reason, and reviewer remain in the history.

How it reads invoices Readable PDFs supply their text directly. Scanned invoices can use local Tesseract OCR when it is available; those results still require human verification. If extraction cannot help, manual review remains available.
05 · Reservation finances & money received

A reservation becomes several financial obligations.

The guest sees the cost of a stay. Behind it, the team may be tracking rent, fees, taxes, deposits, refunds, and money owed to an owner. I wanted those amounts to stay connected to the reservation that explains them.

Reservation folios & revenue

Separate what was charged from what was earned.

The folio connects charges, expected and actual payouts, bank matches, receipts, holds, and documents. Revenue processing uses the effective owner contract to determine timing, such as arrival, checkout, nightly, monthly, or payment received, with a reviewed preview before posting.

Platform payouts & cash application

Explain the deposit as well as the total.

Imported payout reports can be matched to bank activity and allocated across reservations and properties, including fees, refunds, and other deductions. Cash application then connects received money to open balances without applying more than the receipt or the amount owed.

Advance & security deposits

Keep obligations visible.

Guest money received before it is earned remains visible as a liability. Advance-deposit schedules separate received, earned, and outstanding amounts; security-deposit work brings deductions, holds, refund deadlines, and retained notice documents together.

Guest settlements & owner receivables

Keep track of what is still due.

Processor settlement records connect gross receipts, fees, and the net bank deposit. Owner receivables retain applied receipts, aging, collection tasks, and refundable credits, so a remaining balance has a history and a next step.

Property service costs

Completed service orders can be matched to vendor bills. Housekeeping costs can be allocated to the owner, business, guest, or an owner/business split, preserving the rule and source cost behind the allocation.

06 · Intake, cards & source records

The work arrives in more than one format.

A stack of scanned mail, a card statement, and a CSV export ask for different kinds of preparation. I built places to handle that preparation while keeping the source connected to what comes next.

Mailroom Scan Desk

Give each document somewhere to go.

Pages from a bulk scan can be grouped into individual documents, then turned into bill drafts, card receipts, or supporting records for an owner, property, or reservation. The grouping stays inside the workbench.

Card Inbox

Keep the receipt with the charge.

Imported card transactions support receipt review, account and property allocations, approval, posting batches, and statement reconciliation. Missing receipts stay visible, and the statement can close with a supporting packet.

Import Center

Review what an import will change.

Owner and property CSV imports have reusable mappings, row previews, and blocking-error review before records are created or updated. Import history and external references preserve a route back to the source. Bank, card, and payout files have their own accounting import workflows.

Documents & evidence

Let one source support the work it belongs to.

A document can link to several related records. Source history, evidence requirements, review decisions, and extraction corrections remain accessible as the financial work moves between desks.

A familiar place to work

I built shared tables, review panels, and document views, so moving between workspaces does not require learning each one from scratch.

Financial records

Consistent underneath

FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, and PostgreSQL support the records and workflow rules. Versioned migrations carry changes to the data model.

Documents

Proof that stays connected

Documents live in object storage, with links to the records they support. Background work is recorded for a worker to pick up and process.

Related records

08 · Trust accounting & owner funds

A balance is the beginning of a decision.

Money in an account may already have a purpose: a reserve to maintain, a bill to pay, a guest deposit being held, or a refund still to come. HAVEN separates the bank balance from the obligations behind it and preserves the calculation used for an owner’s distribution.

Beneficiary balances

Keep each owner’s money distinct.

Owner and property trust subaccounts retain their balances and immutable activity. Funds checks account for reserves, pending bills and refunds, and withholding. Active unvalued holds block eligibility separately.

Three-way reconciliation

Compare three views of the same money.

Adjusted bank cash, general ledger trust balances, and beneficiary totals are compared directly. Certification requires the variance to be resolved and retains the beneficiary schedule used for the review.

Owner distributions

Show how the available amount was reached.

Distribution runs bring owner items, property allocations, totals, evidence, and approval stages together. Saved calculations keep the basis for a decision available after the run has moved on.

Review & corrections

Keep the decision accountable.

Approval rules are checked behind the interface. Journals must balance before posting; posted entries remain protected, and linked reversals preserve the history when a correction is needed.

09 · Team work & exceptions

Someone else will need to pick this up.

A warning tells the team that something is wrong. Resolving it takes a person, a next step, and enough context to understand the issue. Team Work brings linked tasks, exceptions, approvals, and comments into a queue with ownership, priority, and status visible.

These are shared controls across the system. A missing contract, an unresolved payout difference, and a document still needing review can each have an assigned task, approval request, or discussion linked to the record. Global search and related-record navigation help the next person find both the issue and its context.

10 · Statements, close & reporting

A statement should be able to explain itself.

An owner wants to understand the month. A colleague needs to close it. An accountant may need to examine it later. Those are different views of the same underlying work, and I want each one to lead back to something a person can check.

Owner statements & portal

Make the period understandable.

Statement runs bring income, expenses, management fees, owner charges, reserves, and distributions through review, approval, and publication. Published statements retain a path back to the contract terms used. Owners can view their statements and permitted documents, and upload material for the team to review.

Reconciliation & close

Know what is still unfinished.

Bank and card reconciliation, trust agreement, review tasks, supporting packets, and period locks provide a structured close. The team can see unresolved differences and the work still needed before a period is complete.

Financial reports & forecasts

Look from the portfolio down to the entry.

Reports cover trial balance, account activity, property income and expenses, reservation finances, payout differences, and trust balances. Saved views preserve useful selections. Forecast snapshots keep their sources and assumptions for later comparison with posted results.

Tax & year-end workpapers

Keep preparation connected to its sources.

Period tax calculations, amendments, supporting documents, and remittance records can be prepared and reviewed together. Year-end owner and vendor workpapers retain source payments, withholding, identity checks, and correction history for external filing.

Reports that can leave the screen

Report outputs can be generated as PDF, CSV, or JSON. Audit packets collect the supporting records; archive exports include a manifest of what was preserved. Generated outputs remain linked to their jobs and source context.

11 · Identity, access & reliable operations

Care in the interface needs care underneath it.

Know who is doing the work

The production configuration requires verified identities and multifactor authentication. Organization checks and database row policies restrict which records each company can access.

Give each process only what it needs

The API, background worker, and database maintenance tasks use separate roles. Day-to-day processes do not need the authority to change the schema.

Treat documents as part of the responsibility

Upload checks limit file types and sizes, inspect file signatures, and restrict active content. Temporary access links limit how long a document URL remains usable.

Practice with records made for the purpose

The demonstration uses fictional records and labeled synthetic documents. They let the workflows be exercised without putting a customer’s information on display.

The repository includes checks for authentication, organization boundaries, document handling, independent approval, extraction corrections, and financial controls. The invoice-to-close pilot gives those individual parts a complete path to be tested together.

12 · What I’m working toward

The next person should not have to start from memory.

The part of HAVEN I care most about is what remains understandable after the work has moved on. A payment question may arrive weeks later. A colleague may take over a queue halfway through the day. The documents, decisions, and relationships need to make sense to someone who was not there for every step.

That is the standard I’m building toward: financial work that can be checked, explained, and continued. The portfolio, ledger, reservation, trust, and reporting modules provide the wider foundation. The invoice-to-close pilot is one focused way to test how those parts hold together.
