01 · The Product

Big-city event platforms skip small islands. MatchBook starts there.

On Kauaʻi, community life is rich but scattered: farmers markets in county PDFs, live music on flyers, club meetings passed along by word of mouth. The platforms built for this — Meetup, Eventbrite, Facebook Events — assume metro density and drown a small island in noise from somewhere else.

MatchBook flips the assumption. Every event and group in it is on the island, and the product’s job is matching: learn what someone cares about, put the right three things in front of them, and make “I’m in” one tap.

Product question

What does a community platform look like when it is built for one island instead of every city on earth?

04 · The Brand

Strike up something.

A full identity system drawn around one object: the humble matchbook, where every match is a plan waiting to catch.

The name carries the whole metaphor — a book of matches, a perfect match, striking one. The identity leans into it: a drawn serif wordmark in match orange, hand-textured match illustrations for empty states and moments of delight, and a warm paper-and-charcoal palette that feels more like a favorite café’s flyer wall than a tech product.

Each of the nine interest topics gets its own hand-drawn motif and tint, so a music night, a farmers market, and a trail cleanup read differently at a glance — on cards, calendars, and category walls alike.

05 · Architecture

A reactive full-stack core with server-held rules.

The interface feels instant because the data layer is reactive; it stays trustworthy because every write goes through the server.

Reactivity

Live by default

Meteor publications stream exactly the data each page needs; joins, saves, and edits appear everywhere they matter without a refresh.

Integrity

No client writes

The insecure package is gone. Every mutation is a server method with check-validated arguments, so the swipe deck and the admin dashboard obey the same rules.

Matching

Topics as the contract

Nine interest topics are defined once and drive the category walls, the match deck ordering, the calendar color coding, and the motif system.

Administration

Organize is a surface, not a backdoor

Directory management — groups, events, profiles — runs through the same validated methods behind an admin role gate.

06 · Reflection

Small places deserve software with this much care.

MatchBook is what happens when product thinking, brand illustration, and full-stack engineering are aimed at a community of seventy thousand people instead of a market of seven billion. The constraint is the feature: with one island’s worth of events, matching can be personal, the calendar can be complete, and showing up can actually be the metric.

It is also a study in evolving a codebase — the platform grew out of an earlier campus project, rebuilt surface by surface into a different product with its own identity, audience, and data.
