Choosing a time is only the beginning of making an appointment work.
The starting point

A customer sees an opening on a calendar. Behind that opening are someone’s working hours, the length of a service, a little time between appointments, and the other people who have already booked. A small square on a screen carries quite a lot of responsibility.

That responsibility shaped Chime. I wanted the customer to have a straightforward way to book, and the business to have enough context to carry the appointment through. The two experiences depend on the same details, even when they need to present them very differently.

Putting everything in one product creates its own problem, of course. There is still the risk of making someone learn an entire system just to move one appointment. Much of the design work became a question of what needs to be visible now, what can wait, and what a person should never have to remember for the software.

01 / CHOOSING

Enough to make a decision.

Show the service, duration, price, and available times where the customer is choosing. A deposit should never be mistaken for the whole price.

02 / COMMITTING

A time the business can honor.

An appointment entered by the team takes time off the same availability offered online. Both ways of booking need to agree about what is left.

03 / FOLLOWING THROUGH

Context that stays with the work.

Keep the customer, appointment, deposit, and communication history connected, so the next person can understand what has happened.

For the customer

One decision at a time.

Someone booking an appointment may be fitting it around work, a school pickup, or a dozen other things. The flow separates choosing a service from finding a time, then carries the appointment summary into the remaining steps. They can check what they chose without having to hold it all in their head.

  1. ServiceWhat is it, how long does it take, and what does it cost?
  2. Date & timeWhich of the available openings fits the day?
  3. TermsWhat should I understand before I commit?
  4. ContactHow can the business reach me about this appointment?
  5. DepositIf one is required, how much is due now?
  6. ConfirmationWhat is the appointment status, and how do I add it to my calendar?
For the business

The day keeps moving.

A customer calls to change their time while someone else is checking in. The team needs to see the appointment in the context of the day, understand the consequences of moving it, and know whether the customer still needs to agree. Chime brings that work into the schedule, with a review step before a change is committed.

The decisionWhat changes now and what still needs an answer

Made to belong

It should feel like their business.

Booking is often part of a customer’s first impression. It should carry the business’s voice and identity through to the appointment. I brought the controls for that experience into the studio, beside a preview of what the customer will see.

See the choice while making itAdjust the logo, colors, wording, and service order with a preview close by.
Fit the website that already existsUse an inline booking view, a modal, a floating button, or a hosted booking page.
Know when it is ready to useThe launch checklist checks for actual bookable openings, including services that still need a team member.
Keeping the money understandable

A deposit has a history.

A deposit can be authorized without being collected. It can be released, collected, or partly refunded later. Those distinctions matter to the person paying and to the person explaining the payment. Chime keeps them visible alongside the appointment, with deposits handled through the business’s own connected Stripe account.

  • Show the price as well as the depositThe booking summary distinguishes the service price from the amount required to reserve it.
  • Keep the action close to its contextThe studio supports collection, release, and full or partial refunds with the payment history in view.
  • Follow what actually happenedPayment updates from Stripe are reconciled into the ledger, including failures and refunds.
  • Remember where the money belongsEach deposit retains its original Stripe account, so a later change of payment connection does not erase that history.
The less visible work

The screen has to keep its word.

A clear interface depends on the system following through on what it shows. An open time must still be available when someone books it. A proposed change must remain a proposal until it is accepted. Much of Chime’s engineering is there to keep these ordinary expectations intact.

01 / AVAILABILITY

Check at the moment it matters.

The server checks capacity and booking rules when saving the appointment. The calendar a customer opened a few minutes ago cannot make that decision on its own.

02 / CHANGES

Leave room for an answer.

When a move requires customer approval, the original appointment stays in place. The new time is checked again when the customer accepts.

03 / BOUNDARIES

Keep each business distinct.

Customers, services, schedules, and payment connections belong to a particular business. That relationship is checked in the requests and carried into the data.

04 / RECOVERY

Allow for the imperfect attempt.

Connections drop and people click twice. Payment operations recognize repeated requests, while failed email attempts remain visible and can be retried.

Closing the loop

Did the message go out?

Moving an appointment and telling someone it moved are separate pieces of work. It is easy to finish the first and assume the second happened. Chime keeps a record of email attempts, their status, and any failures, so the team has somewhere to look before sending another message or making another call.

What is waitingQueued messages remain visible until they are processed.
What was sentThe history records successful handoff to the email provider.
What needs attentionFailed attempts carry a reason and a route to retry.
How it soundsThe business can edit and test its customer email templates.
What I built

Both sides, thought through together.

My work on Chime spans the product, its visual identity, the writing inside the interfaces, and the systems that carry a booking through. Working across those parts made their dependencies difficult to ignore: a label in the studio, a sentence in an email, and a rule in the database may all be describing the same decision.

Role
Product design, brand, interface writing, architecture, and full-stack engineering
Product
Customer booking widget, hosted booking page, and business operations studio
For
Service businesses coordinating appointments, team availability, and customers
Focus
Clear decisions, consistent scheduling, understandable payments, and a way to recover when something fails
01

Customer experience

A guided booking flow with service selection, calendar views, terms, contact details, conditional deposits, and confirmation. Responsive layouts and progress cues help the customer keep their place.

02

Business workflow

A studio for the schedule, services, team, customers, payments, and email. Availability publishing and launch checks connect the business’s setup to what a customer can actually book.

03

Shared foundations

React and TypeScript interfaces backed by Node.js, Express, and PostgreSQL. Booking constraints, business boundaries, and Stripe Connect payment operations support both sides of the experience.

04

Checking the joins

Database integration tests, browser accessibility checks, and launch rehearsals cover the places where the parts meet. The project also includes migration, backup, and restore workflows for operating it.

The intention behind Chime

Less to keep in your head.

The work of a service business still belongs to the people doing it. Chime gives that work a place to be recorded, understood, and picked up again: the time that was agreed, the change still waiting for an answer, the deposit that was returned. Small things, until someone has to go looking for them.
